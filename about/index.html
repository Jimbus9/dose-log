Why it exists

I built it for my dad.

My dad has diabetes, and a few of us in the family help out. Before this, keeping track meant screenshots, texts and phone calls: has he had his long-acting today? What did he eat? What did the nurse say?

Several of us took him to appointments, and each time the news had to be passed round by word of mouth. Everyone needed the same information, and nobody had all of it.

The apps that already exist are either built for the person managing their own diabetes, with graphs and settings everywhere, or built for carers. Nothing was simple enough for Dad to use and shared enough for the rest of us.

Dad taps a big button. The rest of us see it straight away. That's the whole idea. Everything else is there to support it.

What it does

A few features, chosen on purpose.

Second-dose warning

Tap long-acting twice in one day and it asks first. The family gets a flag if it hasn't been logged by a set time.

Reminders

A notification on their phone if a meal or dose hasn't been logged yet. Nothing if it has.

Glucose graph

Optional readings from LibreLinkUp, with doses and meals marked on the same timeline.

Shared calendar

Appointments in one place. The home screen shows the next one coming up.

Checked by family

Admins can mark each dose as seen and agreed, and get an alert when a new one is logged.

Private by design

Only emails on your family list can sign in. The database enforces that on every request.

!

A log, not a doctor.

Dose Log only records what the person says they took. It never suggests, calculates or adjusts a dose, and it never will. Always follow your diabetes team's plan.

It isn't a medical device, and it comes with no warranty.

Use it yourself

Your copy, your data.

Dose Log isn't a service you sign up to. It's free, open-source code you run for your own household, so your family's health information stays in your own account and nobody else's.
